About Kilograms per square metre per second

The kilogram per square metre per second (kg/(m²·s)) is the SI coherent unit of mass flux density, equal to 1 kg·m⁻²·s⁻¹. It appears directly in the continuity equation (ṁ = ρv) and in Stefan's law for evaporation. Rocket nozzle throat mass flux can reach 500-2000 kg/(m²·s); convective heat exchangers: 100-500 kg/(m²·s). 1 kg/(m²·s) = 1 000 g/(m²·s).

About Kilograms per square centimetre per second

The kilogram per square centimetre per second (kg/(cm²·s)) equals 10⁴ kg/(m²·s) (exact: 1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m²). It is used for extreme high-pressure mass-flux situations such as detonation physics, shaped-charge jet penetration, and hypervelocity impact analysis. 1 kg/(cm²·s) = 10⁴ kg/(m²·s) = 1 000 g/(cm²·s).
