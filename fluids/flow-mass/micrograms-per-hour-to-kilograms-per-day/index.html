About Micrograms per hour

The microgram per hour (µg/h) equals 10⁻⁹/3600 ≈ 2.778×10⁻¹³ kg/s, used in corrosion science to quantify mass loss rates of thin films or passive oxide layers. A stainless steel specimen losing 1 µg/h represents an extremely low corrosion rate typical of passive surfaces in neutral aqueous environments. 1 µg/h = 1/3600 µg/s ≈ 2.778×10⁻¹³ kg/s.

About Kilograms per day

The kilogram per day (kg/d) equals 1/86400 ≈ 1.157×10⁻⁵ kg/s, standard in environmental engineering for pollutant load calculations and water-treatment material dosing. Activated-carbon dosing for a water-treatment plant, sludge dewatering output, and river sediment transport surveys all use kg/d. A medium river in flood may move 10 000-100 000 kg/d of suspended solids. 1 kg/d ≈ 1.157×10⁻⁵ kg/s.
