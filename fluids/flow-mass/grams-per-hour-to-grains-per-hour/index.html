About Grams per hour

The gram per hour (g/h) equals 10⁻³/3600 ≈ 2.778×10⁻⁷ kg/s, used in fuel-cell hydrogen consumption tests, precision agricultural sprayers, and humidity calibration. A 1 kW PEM fuel cell consumes roughly 750 g/h of hydrogen at rated power. Automotive air-conditioning refrigerant leak standards are set in g/h. 1 g/h ≈ 2.778×10⁻⁷ kg/s = 1/3600 g/s.

About Grains per hour

The grain per hour (gr/h) equals 6.479891×10⁻⁵/3600 ≈ 1.800×10⁻⁸ kg/s, used in HVAC psychrometric calculations for dehumidifier capacity and moisture-removal rates. ASHRAE load calculation spreadsheets report latent loads in grains of water per hour; a typical residential dehumidifier removes 5×10⁵-2×10⁶ gr/h. 1 gr/h ≈ 1.800×10⁻⁸ kg/s.
