About Degrees per minute

The degree per minute (°/min) equals π/10800 rad/s ≈ 2.909 × 10⁻⁴ rad/s. The sidereal tracking rate for astronomical telescopes is 0.25 °/min (15 °/h); modern robotic observatories slew at 100-400 °/min between targets. A clock's hour hand turns at 0.5 °/min; the minute hand at 6 °/min. Industrial drum conveyors for cement kilns rotate at 0.5-5 °/min; automotive power-steering columns during parking manoeuvres rotate at 30-180 °/min. 1 °/min ≈ 2.909 × 10⁻⁴ rad/s = 1/60 rpm.

About Revolutions per day

The revolution per day (rev/d) equals π/43200 rad/s ≈ 7.272 × 10⁻⁵ rad/s, the scale of planetary rotation and orbital mechanics. Earth spins at 1.00274 rev/day (sidereal); Mars at 1.027 rev/day (sol = 24 h 37 min); Jupiter at 2.417 rev/day. Geostationary satellites orbit at exactly 1 rev/solar day; Molniya HEO satellites at 2 rev/day. The Moon is tidally locked at 1/27.322 rev/day. IERS Bulletin A tracks Earth's deviation from 1 rev/solar day to 0.1 ms precision. 1 rev/d ≈ 7.272 × 10⁻⁵ rad/s ≈ 6.944 × 10⁻³ rpm.
