About Kilocoulombs

The kilocoulomb (kC) equals 10³ C ≈ 0.278 Ah, used in industrial electrochemistry, large capacitor bank energy calculations, and fault current analysis. Electroplating a 100 cm² nickel layer 25 µm thick requires roughly 2-4 kC; power-factor-correction capacitor banks in substations may store several kilocoulombs. 1 kC = 10⁻³ MC = 10³ C.

About Attocoulombs

The attocoulomb (aC) equals 10⁻¹⁸ C, sitting between the elementary charge and the femtocoulomb. It arises in ultra-sensitive charge measurements: single-electron transistors, quantum capacitance probing of graphene and 2D materials, and scanning tunnelling microscopy where tip-sample charge exchange is tracked at this scale. 1 aC = 10⁻³ fC ≈ 6.24 e.
