About Barns

A barn (b) is a unit of area used in nuclear and particle physics to measure effective cross-sections of atomic nuclei and particle interaction probabilities. Equal to 10⁻²⁸ m² (10⁻²⁴ cm²), it was humorously named during the Manhattan Project - physicists joked that uranium nuclei were 'as big as a barn' at the subatomic scale. Particle physics experiments at accelerators like CERN report cross-sections in millibarns (mb), microbarns (μb), nanobarns (nb), and femtobarns (fb).

About Square Centimeters

A square centimeter (cm²) is a metric unit of area equal to a square with sides of one centimeter (10 mm × 10 mm). It is widely used for measuring moderate-sized surfaces like postage stamps, credit cards, or smartphone screens. A standard credit card has an area of about 46 cm². This unit is common in everyday crafts, school science, and smaller construction projects where precision matters but square meters would be too large.
