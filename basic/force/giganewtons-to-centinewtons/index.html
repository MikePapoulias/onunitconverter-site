About Giganewtons

One giganewton (GN) is 10⁹ N ≈ 100,000 metric tonnes-force - the scale of geophysically significant forces. Earth-Moon gravitational attraction is ~198 GN, driving tidal forces. The Himalayan collision zone transmits hundreds of GN per km of plate boundary. The Hoover Dam's structural dead load is ~67 GN. For comparison, the entire Saturn V launch thrust was only 33.4 MN = 0.033 GN, illustrating that giganewtons are truly planetary in scale. 1 GN = 1,000 MN = 10⁹ N = 224,800 kips.

About Centinewtons

A centinewton (cN) is exactly 0.01 N - the standard force unit of the textile industry. ISO and ASTM specs give fibre tenacity in cN/tex (centinewtons per tex, where 1 tex = 1 g/km): high-tenacity polyester 70-80 cN/tex; Kevlar/Twaron para-aramid 200-250 cN/tex; Dyneema/Spectra UHMWPE >350 cN/tex. Sewing thread breaking strength is 200-800 cN. Packaging tensile testers, adhesive peel-force instruments, and fine spring calibrators all report in cN. 1 cN ≈ 1 pond (0.980665 cN). 100 cN = 1 N.
