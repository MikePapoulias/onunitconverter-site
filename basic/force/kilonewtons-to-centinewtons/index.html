About Kilonewtons

A kilonewton (kN) is 1,000 N - the standard force unit for structural engineering outside North America. A 70 kg person exerts ~0.687 kN; a heavy goods vehicle axle 100-130 kN; a loaded Boeing 747 on its landing gear ~2,800 kN. Eurocode and BS EN structural codes express beam loads and foundation reactions in kN and bending moments in kN·m. Universal testing machines for materials testing are rated 10-10,000 kN. Prestressed concrete tendons are stressed to 100-250 kN each. 1 kN = 1,000 N = 224.81 lbf = 101.97 kgf.

About Centinewtons

A centinewton (cN) is exactly 0.01 N - the standard force unit of the textile industry. ISO and ASTM specs give fibre tenacity in cN/tex (centinewtons per tex, where 1 tex = 1 g/km): high-tenacity polyester 70-80 cN/tex; Kevlar/Twaron para-aramid 200-250 cN/tex; Dyneema/Spectra UHMWPE >350 cN/tex. Sewing thread breaking strength is 200-800 cN. Packaging tensile testers, adhesive peel-force instruments, and fine spring calibrators all report in cN. 1 cN ≈ 1 pond (0.980665 cN). 100 cN = 1 N.
