About Sidereal Days

A sidereal day = Earth's rotation period relative to the stars = 23 h 56 min 4.0905 s = 86,164.0905 s - ~3 min 56 s shorter than the 24 h solar day because Earth advances ~1° in its orbit each day. Equatorial telescope mounts rotate at one turn per sidereal day to track stars. VLBI, GPS orbit determination, and polar-motion monitoring use sidereal rotation as their reference. 1 sid. day = 86,164.0905 s.

About Sidereal Months

The sidereal month = Moon's orbital period relative to the fixed stars = 27.3217 days = 2,360,591.6 s - the shortest lunar month. It is ~2.21 days shorter than the synodic month (29.53 d) because Earth advances ~27° along its orbit in the same period; the Moon must travel further to re-align with the Sun. Used in GPS orbit design, tidal modelling, and lunar laser ranging. 1 sid.mo = 27.3217 d.
