About Pascals

The pascal (Pa) is the SI unit of pressure - 1 N/m², named after Blaise Pascal (1623-1662). Standard sea-level atmosphere ≈ 101,325 Pa. One pascal is very small: roughly the pressure of a single banknote lying flat. Pascal is simultaneously the SI unit for mechanical stress and elastic modulus: structural steel Young's modulus ~200 GPa; rubber shear modulus ~0.5 MPa. Used universally in fluid mechanics, acoustics, atmospheric science, and materials engineering. 1 Pa = 1 N/m².

About Bars

A bar = exactly 100,000 Pa ≈ 0.987 atm - the world standard for industrial pressure outside North America. Hydraulic systems, pneumatic equipment, gas cylinders, dive computers, and tyre gauges in Europe and Asia use bar. Scuba diving: +1 bar per 10 m depth; at 40 m recreational limit = 5 bar absolute. European tyre pressure labels show bar alongside kPa. Industrial processes: steam at 10-50 bar; high-pressure reactors 100-300 bar; water-jet cutting 1,000-4,000 bar. 1 bar = 100,000 Pa = 14.504 psi.
