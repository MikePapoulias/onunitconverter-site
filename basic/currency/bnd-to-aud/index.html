About Bruneian Dollar

The Bruneian Dollar (BND) is the official currency of Brunei Darussalam, introduced in 1967 when Brunei separated its currency from the unified Malaya and British Borneo dollar. By a Currency Interchangeability Agreement signed in 1967, the BND is interchangeable at par (1:1) with the Singapore Dollar (SGD) - both currencies are accepted in both countries. The Autoriti Monetari Brunei Darussalam manages the currency. Brunei's economy is dominated by oil and natural gas exports, which give it one of the highest per capita incomes in Asia. The dollar is divided into 100 sen.

About Australian Dollar

The Australian Dollar (AUD), nicknamed the "Aussie", was introduced on 14 February 1966 when Australia decimalised its currency, replacing the Australian pound at two dollars to the pound. It is the fifth most traded currency in global foreign exchange markets, reflecting Australia's status as a major commodity exporter - particularly iron ore, coal, and gold. The Reserve Bank of Australia manages monetary policy. The AUD is also legal tender in several Pacific island nations including Kiribati, Nauru, and Tuvalu.
