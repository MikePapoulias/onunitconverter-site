About Pounds

The avoirdupois pound (lb) is the primary unit of weight in the United States and United Kingdom for everyday use, equal to 16 ounces or exactly 453.59237 grams. It dominates American life: body weight, grocery produce, deli counter cuts, freight shipping costs, gym weights, and building material quantities are all measured in pounds and ounces. American recipes use pounds for meat and large quantities. The pound derives from the Roman 'libra pondo' (a pound by weight) - hence both the symbol 'lb' (from libra) and the British currency symbol '£'.

About Micrograms

A microgram (µg or mcg) is one millionth of a gram (10⁻⁶ g). It is the standard dosing unit for potent medications and micronutrients: vitamin B12 recommended daily intake is 2.4 µg, folic acid supplements are 400 µg, and levothyroxine (thyroid medication) doses are typically 25-200 µg. Air quality standards measure fine particulate matter (PM2.5) in micrograms per cubic meter - the WHO guideline is 15 µg/m³ annual mean. A grain of sand weighs about 50-100 micrograms, and a single snowflake approximately 3 micrograms.
