About Stones

A stone (st) equals 14 pounds or approximately 6.350 kg. It is the standard unit for expressing human body weight in the United Kingdom and Ireland, where a person will say they weigh '11 stone 4' (meaning 11 stone and 4 pounds, or 158 lb / 71.7 kg). Despite the UK's official adoption of the metric system, stones remain the dominant unit for personal body weight on British bathroom scales, in doctors' offices, and in everyday conversation. British newspapers and sports reports routinely describe athletes' weights in stones and pounds.

About Micrograms

A microgram (µg or mcg) is one millionth of a gram (10⁻⁶ g). It is the standard dosing unit for potent medications and micronutrients: vitamin B12 recommended daily intake is 2.4 µg, folic acid supplements are 400 µg, and levothyroxine (thyroid medication) doses are typically 25-200 µg. Air quality standards measure fine particulate matter (PM2.5) in micrograms per cubic meter - the WHO guideline is 15 µg/m³ annual mean. A grain of sand weighs about 50-100 micrograms, and a single snowflake approximately 3 micrograms.
