About Microjoules

A microjoule (µJ) is 10⁻⁶ J - the scale of precision laser ablation, ESD damage events, and individual LIDAR pulses. LASIK eye surgery delivers 0.5-2 µJ per pulse at 193 nm, ablating ~0.25 µm of corneal tissue per pulse. ESD events that destroy IC gates correspond to 1-1,000 µJ; the Human Body Model test discharges ~500 µJ at 2 kV. Q-switched microchip laser pulses for rangefinding are 1-100 µJ. The kinetic energy of a 1 mg grain of sand falling 10 cm is mgh ≈ 0.98 µJ. 1 µJ = 10⁻⁶ J.

About Ergs

An erg is the CGS unit of energy - the work done by one dyne through one centimetre - exactly 10⁻⁷ J. Despite SI adoption, the erg persists in astrophysics: the Sun's luminosity is 3.828 × 10³³ erg/s; gamma-ray burst isotropic energies are quoted in units of 10⁵¹ erg ('one foe'). Surface tension of water is 72.8 dyn/cm; the work to create 1 cm² of new water-air interface is 72.8 erg. Earthquake seismic moment M₀ is in dyn·cm = erg; the 2004 Boxing Day earthquake had M₀ ≈ 10²³ J = 10³⁰ erg. 1 erg = 10⁻⁷ J = 624.15 eV.
