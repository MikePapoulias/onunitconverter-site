About Nanojoules

A nanojoule (nJ) is 10⁻⁹ J - the scale of ultrashort laser pulses, flash memory write operations, and cellular ATP reserves. A Ti:sapphire laser oscillator produces pulses of 1-20 nJ at 80 MHz. Flash NAND programming costs ~1-10 nJ/bit written; a read is ~0.1 nJ/bit. The total ATP energy stored in a single red blood cell is ~1-5 nJ. The kinetic energy of a 1 µg particle at 1.4 m/s is ~1 nJ, illustrating that nanojoules describe real nanoscale mechanical events. 1 nJ = 10⁻⁹ J.

About Terajoules

A terajoule (TJ) is 10¹² J - the scale of nuclear yields in the kiloton range and large industrial accidents. One kiloton of TNT = 4.184 TJ; the Hiroshima bomb (15 kt) released ~62.8 TJ. The Halifax Explosion (1917) released ~3 TJ. A 500 MW coal plant consumes ~51.4 TJ/h of thermal input. Daily primary energy of Denmark is ~250-300 TJ; the UK ~3,800 TJ/day. A 1 GW nuclear reactor at 90% capacity factor generates ~77.7 TJ/day. 1 TJ = 10¹² J = 277,778 kWh = 277.78 MWh = 23.88 ktoe.
