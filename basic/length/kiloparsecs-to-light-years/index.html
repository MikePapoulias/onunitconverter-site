About Kiloparsecs

A kiloparsec (kpc) is 1,000 parsecs or 3,260 light years, making it ideal for measuring distances within galaxies. Our Milky Way galaxy has a diameter of about 30-40 kiloparsecs, and the Sun is located roughly 8 kiloparsecs from the galactic center. Star clusters within our galaxy are typically measured in kiloparsecs. The galactic halo extends several kiloparsecs beyond the visible disk. This unit helps astronomers discuss galactic structures and the distribution of stars, gas, and dark matter within galaxies.

About Light Years

A light year (ly) is the distance light travels in one year through a vacuum, approximately 9.46 trillion kilometers. This is the primary unit for measuring stellar distances. Our nearest star neighbor, Proxima Centauri, is 4.24 light years away, meaning its light takes over 4 years to reach us. The center of our Milky Way galaxy is about 26,000 light years distant, and the nearest large galaxy (Andromeda) is 2.5 million light years away. When we observe distant objects, we're literally seeing them as they were years ago.
