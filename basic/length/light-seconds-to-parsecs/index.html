About Light Seconds

A light second (ls) is the distance light travels in one second through a vacuum, exactly 299,792,458 meters. This represents one of the fundamental constants in physics - the speed of light. For perspective, light takes about 1.3 seconds to travel from Earth to the Moon, and 8.3 minutes (500 light seconds) to reach us from the Sun. This unit is crucial in astronomy, telecommunications (satellite delays), and physics experiments, helping us understand the finite speed of light and its implications for communication and observation.

About Parsecs

A parsec (pc) is approximately 3.26 light years or 30.86 trillion kilometers, defined as the distance at which one astronomical unit subtends an angle of one arcsecond. Despite being less familiar than light years, parsecs are preferred by professional astronomers because they relate directly to stellar parallax measurements - the apparent shift in a star's position as Earth orbits the Sun. Most nearby stars are within a few dozen parsecs, our galaxy is about 30,000 parsecs across, and distant galaxies are measured in millions of parsecs (megaparsecs).
