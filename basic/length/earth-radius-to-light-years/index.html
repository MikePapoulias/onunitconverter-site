About Earth Radius

The Earth radius (R⊕) is approximately 6.371 million meters or 6,371 kilometers, representing the average distance from Earth's center to its surface. Due to Earth's rotation, it's slightly flattened, with the equatorial radius being about 21 km larger than the polar radius. This fundamental measurement is crucial in geology, geography, and astronomy for calculating Earth's circumference (about 40,000 km), surface area, and volume. It's also used as a comparative unit for describing other planets and exoplanets.

About Light Years

A light year (ly) is the distance light travels in one year through a vacuum, approximately 9.46 trillion kilometers. This is the primary unit for measuring stellar distances. Our nearest star neighbor, Proxima Centauri, is 4.24 light years away, meaning its light takes over 4 years to reach us. The center of our Milky Way galaxy is about 26,000 light years distant, and the nearest large galaxy (Andromeda) is 2.5 million light years away. When we observe distant objects, we're literally seeing them as they were years ago.
