About Roentgens

The roentgen (R) equals exactly 2.58 × 10⁻⁴ C/kg, the historical unit of X-ray and gamma-ray exposure defined in 1928 (ICRU) and redefined exactly in 1962. Still widely used in the US: field survey instruments read in R/h; NRC and EPA regulations cite R and mR; the Chernobyl accounts famously reported readings in R/h. 1 R = 2.58 × 10⁻⁴ C/kg.

About Femtocoulombs per kilogram

The femtocoulomb per kilogram (fC/kg) equals 10⁻¹⁵ C/kg. Used in computational dosimetry to characterise the ionisation yield from single low-energy photon interactions in nanogram air volumes; ultra-sensitive free-air ionisation chamber simulations in microdosimetry research report air-ionisation at the fC/kg level. 1 fC/kg = 10⁻¹⁵ C/kg = 10⁻³ pC/kg.
