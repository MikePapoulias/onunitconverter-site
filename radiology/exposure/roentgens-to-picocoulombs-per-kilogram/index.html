About Roentgens

The roentgen (R) equals exactly 2.58 × 10⁻⁴ C/kg, the historical unit of X-ray and gamma-ray exposure defined in 1928 (ICRU) and redefined exactly in 1962. Still widely used in the US: field survey instruments read in R/h; NRC and EPA regulations cite R and mR; the Chernobyl accounts famously reported readings in R/h. 1 R = 2.58 × 10⁻⁴ C/kg.

About Picocoulombs per kilogram

The picocoulomb per kilogram (pC/kg) equals 10⁻¹² C/kg. Environmental gamma exposure rates in very low background areas approach pC/kg/h; precision ionisation chamber calibration laboratories measure leakage and secondary ionisation currents in terms of pC/kg. 1 pC/kg = 10⁻¹² C/kg = 10⁻³ nC/kg.
