About Roentgens

The roentgen (R) equals exactly 2.58 × 10⁻⁴ C/kg, the historical unit of X-ray and gamma-ray exposure defined in 1928 (ICRU) and redefined exactly in 1962. Still widely used in the US: field survey instruments read in R/h; NRC and EPA regulations cite R and mR; the Chernobyl accounts famously reported readings in R/h. 1 R = 2.58 × 10⁻⁴ C/kg.

About Kiloroentgens

The kiloroentgen (kR) equals 0.258 C/kg. Used in radiation hardness testing of electronics and legacy nuclear weapons effects literature; total ionising exposure inside nuclear facilities during design-basis accident scenarios is cited in kR; some older radiation therapy machine output specifications use kR/min. 1 kR = 0.258 C/kg = 10³ R.
